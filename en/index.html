01

An overlooked emission source

Plants clean the water. The sludge stays a problem.

Conventional sludge treatment is demanding in energy, land and chemicals, and releases gases many times more potent than CO₂. The result is often waste that has to be incinerated or landfilled.

25×

Methane from digestion

Digesters release CH₄, with 25 times the global warming potential of CO₂.

298×

Nitrous oxide

Nitrification and denitrification produce N₂O, almost 300 times more potent than CO₂.

75%

Power for aeration

Up to three quarters of a plant's electricity goes to aerating activated sludge. Drying and incineration additionally require heat or steam.

30–50m

Land and buffer zones

Drying beds, composting areas and sanitary buffer zones require extensive land. Many processes also use coagulants and disinfectants.

02

The solution

Sludge turns into a product inside a closed reactor.

Enzyme-cavitation technology combines aerobic stabilisation, enzymatic breakdown and low-intensity hydrodynamic cavitation in vertical reactors. Details are in the sections below.
