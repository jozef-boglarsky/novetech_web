Enzyme-cavitation technology

Oxygen, cavitation and enzymes in one vertical vessel.

The process combines aerobic stabilisation, enzymatic breakdown and low-intensity hydrodynamic cavitation. The process elements have no moving parts; the mixture is circulated by pumps.

01
1 · oxyjet – ejector nozzle, O₂ saturation 2 · turbojet – cavitation generator 3 · circulation pump

  1. Intake and homogenisation

    Sludge enters a closed vessel where it is mixed with residual activated sludge and prepared for treatment.

  2. Aerobic stabilisation

    The vertical reactor layout ensures high partial pressure. Ejector oxyjets finely disperse air, so dissolved oxygen exceeds 15 mg/l.

  3. Low-intensity cavitation

    A stationary turbojet turns laminar flow into turbulent flow. Cavitation ruptures microbial cell walls and activates enzymes.

  4. Biological breakdown

    Exo- and endo-fermentative oxidation breaks down 30–45 % of organic matter, mostly fats (about 70 %) and proteins (about 30 %). The exothermic reaction keeps the mixture at 25–40 °C without external heating.

  5. Sanitisation and detoxification

    The process eliminates pathogens and parasite eggs, degrades PCBs, pesticides, phenols and antibiotics, and converts heavy-metal salts into stable natural forms.

  6. Output: a product, not waste

    The thickened sludge is dewatered without flocculants. Sludge water with BOD₅ below 100 mg/l returns to the process. The result is a stable, odourless peat-like substrate.
